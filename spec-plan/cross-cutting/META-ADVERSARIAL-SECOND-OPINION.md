## 1. Method critique — where parallel authoring produced hidden errors

The orchestration (`00-ORCHESTRATION-PLAN.md`) is a clean fan-out: orchestrator → 6 domain leads → author/adversarial/alt subagents → a Wave-2 cross-cutting wave of **5 independent agents** (`MASTER-PLAN`, `MASTER-PLAN-ALT`, `CROSSCUT-ADVERSARIAL`, `DATA-MODEL`, `TRACEABILITY`). That last sentence is the root cause of the most serious defect I found: **the five Wave-2 agents ran in parallel and were never reconciled against each other.** The method has no Wave-3 step that cross-checks the cross-cutting documents for mutual consistency — only a "primary writes the master index, runs a consistency pass" (Plan §7, Wave 3), which clearly did not catch the following.

### M-1 (CRITICAL): The cross-cutting wave contradicts itself about whether C2 is frozen.
This is the single highest-leverage finding of *this* pass — the mirror image of CROSSCUT-ADVERSARIAL's own headline.

- `CROSSCUT-ADVERSARIAL.md` (the "final hostile pass") concludes, as its #1 finding and its entire bottom line: **C2 v1.0 was frozen too early and must be un-frozen to `v1.0-rc`** to add a `disposition` axis + `obligations[]` array, regenerate the action enums, make external-data value capture a MUST, etc. (XD-1, XD-2, XD-3, XD-11, XD-12, XD-13; §4 build-blocking table; §5 bottom line lines 489-493).
- `MASTER-PLAN.md`, authored in parallel, declares **FC-1, FC-2, FC-3 `FROZEN`** (table lines 28-30) and frames the action-model question as something still to "resolve … **before C2 bakes it**" (line 211, W0-d) — written as if C2 has *not yet* baked it. It never once says "un-freeze," "re-open," or "v1.0-rc." Its entire 5-wave plan and critical path assume the frozen 36-field schema is correct.
- `INTER-DOMAIN-CONTRACTS.md` goes further and asserts the *opposite* of XD-3: Contract 5 §5.5 says the disposition/obligations split is **"purely semantic and needs no schema bump … no new C2 field is required."** XD-3 says precisely that a new field *is* required because `mutate` sits as a sibling of `deny` in the flat enum with no `disposition`/`obligations[]` home. **These two cross-cutting documents give directly opposite instructions to the C2 and B4 build teams.**
- `DATA-MODEL.md` (line 14, line 91) and every `DOMAIN-SUMMARY.md` (C-domain §3 line 21 republishes the flat `decision` enum verbatim and tells everyone "code against this today, it won't break") treat the frozen schema as authoritative and correct.

**Net:** four of the six cross-cutting docs say "C2 is frozen and right"; one says "C2 is frozen and *wrong*, un-freeze it"; one (`INTER-DOMAIN-CONTRACTS`) tries to split disposition/obligations but does it over the *existing* fields and explicitly forbids the schema change that the split actually requires. A build team handed this corpus does not know whether to freeze or re-open C2. **The reconciliation did not reconcile.** The `00-MASTER-INDEX.md` "Headline reconciliation flags" (lines 113-119) do flag the C2 re-open — which means the *index author* knew, but never propagated the correction back into MASTER-PLAN/DATA-MODEL/INTER-DOMAIN-CONTRACTS/the domain summaries. The flag sits in the index; the load-bearing planning docs still say FROZEN.

### M-2: "Convergent agreement" is being used as evidence of correctness, and it is not.
The corpus repeatedly cites *two independent agents reaching the same answer* as validation: `best_effort` rename "convergently reached by two independent agents" (`00-MASTER-INDEX.md` line 128); D2's `ScopePredicate` indirection invented in two places; etc. In a parallel fan-out **seeded from the same source spec and the same persona/scenario corpus**, convergence is the *expected* outcome of shared priors, not independent confirmation. It is correlated error, not corroboration. The places where agents *converged* (the honesty thesis, the replay-is-authoritative thesis, "OPA decides / engines effect") are exactly the places that received the *least* genuine adversarial pressure, because every agent already believed them. See §2.

### M-3: The "frozen" framing was applied to a schema before its consumers existed.
C2 §3.13 declares the schema FROZEN and the C-domain summary calls freezing-early "the right call" because consumers block on it (C-summary §4 decision 1). But XD-3/XD-11/XD-12 are all defects discovered *by the consumers* (E1, C3, B4, D1) — i.e. the freeze happened before the people who would find the bugs had finished looking. Freezing a contract to unblock parallel work is sound; *declaring it FROZEN rather than `v1.0-rc-frozen-pending-consumer-signoff`* is a process error that the corpus then had to spend its entire adversarial budget unwinding. The lesson the corpus half-learned (un-freeze to rc) was never fed back into its own method.

### M-4: Orchestration decisions D-001..D-004 are thinner than advertised, and one is load-bearing.
`DECISIONS.md` only contains **D-001 through D-004** (I checked — the index's "D-001..D-015" framing in your brief overstates what exists; there are four). Of these, **D-001 (exclude `.claude/skills/**` and `retrospective/**` as "tooling, not product")** is defensible but quietly removed the one place where *operational/human-factors* concerns would have lived. The product's biggest unaddressed risks (who writes Rego, who runs the stack — §3 below) fall in exactly the "how we work" bucket D-001 excluded. The decision was right for scoping the *doc corpus* and wrong as a silent excuse for the *product* to never own those risks. D-002 (scenarios are inputs, not re-spec'd) is fine. D-003/D-004 are process. **There is no decision log entry for the most consequential unattended choice in the whole corpus: that C2 should be declared FROZEN.** A choice that the cross-cut then overturned does not appear in the decision log at all.

## 2. Groupthink / unchallenged assumptions

Each of these was accepted by essentially every agent. For each: is it true, and what would falsify it?

### G-1: "OPA decides, engines effect" (decision/effector separation, R-B4-2, B-summary hardest-decision #1).
Treated as *the* architectural keystone that makes the platform engine-portable. **Largely true but oversold.** It holds cleanly for OPA→Gatekeeper (both consume Rego/CEL-ish logic). It does **not** hold for Kyverno, which the B-domain itself selects as the *effector* for "complex/identity/replay" cases (B-summary decision #2: "OPA decisions + Kyverno effects … Kyverno closes complex/identity/replay gap"). Kyverno does not execute OPA decisions — it has its own YAML policy language and its own admission logic. So the real architecture is "OPA decides *for the cases OPA can express*, and for the rest a second engine both decides and effects" — which is a materially weaker portability claim than "one Rego decides everywhere" (the exact phrase MASTER-PLAN §5.1 line 296 uses to define the defensible core). **Falsifier:** take the flagship image-signing control and express it once in Rego, then show Kyverno *effecting* that identical decision without re-authoring policy logic in Kyverno's language. The corpus never demonstrates this; B1-R30's cross-engine conformance suite is named as the proof but listed as "highest-value test" still to be built (MASTER-PLAN §7.6). The portability thesis is **unproven and is the load-bearing claim of the whole product.**

### G-2: "Replay is authoritative evidence" (the central differentiator).
Accepted everywhere; it is the product's reason to exist. The corpus's *own* adversarial work shows this is true for a **narrow slice and best_effort or worse for most real cases**: XD-1 shows the flagship image-signing example replays `non-complete` because nobody captures the external-data *value*; XD-2 shows the same event can be `complete` for one bundle and `insufficient` for another (policy-relative completeness); XD-13 shows a stale dependency catalog yields confidently-*wrong* `complete`. So "authoritative replay" is real only when (value captured) AND (catalog current) AND (bundle hasn't grown a new input) AND (policy is deterministic) AND (no PII redaction, which XD-17 notes is *mandatory* in exactly the regulated namespaces where replay is most valuable). **Falsifier / the test the corpus should run before selling this:** sample 1,000 real production decisions across payments-prod and count what fraction replay `complete` end-to-end. If it is not a large majority, the marketing claim ("authoritative replay") is a `best_effort` product wearing a `complete` label — which is precisely the "declared vs verified" sin (XD-6) the platform exists to prevent. **Nobody owns measuring this number.** It should be a gating acceptance criterion, not a footnote.

### G-3: "Storage-layer authz is achievable in the POC" (D2 §1.3, §17A.1, the domain's signature invariant).
The corpus is admirably honest that this collides with deferred storage (XD-5, F-summary hardest-decision #1) and resolves it with "a linkable `ScopePredicate` library over ordinary storage." **The unexamined assumption is that an app-layer mandatory query-rewrite over BYO/ordinary storage is genuinely a control and not a lint.** D2-A1 (cited in XD-5) already concedes the choke point is "enforced by a lint, not a control; one bypass voids it." The resolution promotes RLS-under-interceptor "from ALT to mandatory" for relational — but C2's *recommended* store (C2-OQ1) is an **append-only object-store log + index DB**, which is *not* relational and has *no* RLS. So the strongest enforcement mechanism (RLS) doesn't exist for the storage backend the evidence spine actually wants. The aggregate-read path (C3/C5 analytics, XD-5) is the richest data and the most likely to bypass. **Falsifier:** DT-55 scope-isolation pen-test against the *object-store-log* backend (not a Postgres demo). If scope can only be enforced over the relational read-model and not over the system-of-record log, then a direct read of the log is a P0 scope escape by construction. The POC plan tests scope isolation (MASTER-PLAN §5.2) but does not state *which backend* it tests it on.

### G-4: "Headlamp plugin is the right console" (E2, §16).
E2's own adversarial review (E2-A3, E2-D11) flags Headlamp as **CNCF sandbox-stage with no plugin-API stability guarantee** — and then rates the risk "Low" and proceeds. For a product whose entire value proposition is *auditor-trustable evidence over a 5-10 year compliance horizon*, betting the primary human surface on a sandbox project's unstable plugin API is not Low risk; it is a multi-year maintenance liability that competitors (Backstage, standalone) don't carry. The "isolate Headlamp-specific code / keep backend portable" mitigation is correct but turns the console into a thin client over a portable backend — at which point the question "why Headlamp at all vs. a standalone React app we control" is never honestly asked. **Falsifier:** project the cost of one Headlamp major-version plugin-API break across the 5-year support commitment the FINOS/regulated buyer (Stack C) requires. If it's material, "Headlamp-first" is a demo-velocity choice masquerading as an architecture choice.

### G-5: The differentiation thesis ("no OSS product offers differential sim + lineage graph + uniform PDP", MASTER-PLAN §5.1 line 296).
Reality-checked against the market memos: this is **true today but the memos themselves undercut it.** `reframed market position.md` says "almost every horizontal layer already has a strong incumbent" and that the only uncontested ground is "the connective tissue" — and that **Stack A (the connective-tissue company) is "the most defensible long term but slow to monetize because every customer also runs ten other tools and your value is 'across' them"** (line 172). The platform-first MASTER-PLAN builds the *whole* connective-tissue stack before selling anything — which the memo explicitly calls the **weakest** bet ("maximizes the time spent on the one bet the research says is weakest", MASTER-PLAN-ALT line 234). So the corpus contains its own market rebuttal of its own primary plan, and resolves it only by offering MASTER-PLAN-ALT as a co-equal "alternative" rather than confronting that **the market memo favors ALT and disfavors the primary.** The groupthink here is *engineering-led sequencing presented as neutral* when the business evidence points the other way. **Falsifier:** name one design partner who will pay for the connective tissue before E2's lineage graph exists. If none, platform-first is building 23 components on spec.

### G-6: "Honesty over coverage" as a sufficient integrity guarantee (C-domain through-line).
This is the corpus's most admirable principle and I do not want to undersell it — surfacing `insufficient`/`best_effort`/`indeterminate` as first-class states is genuinely the right call. **The unchallenged assumption is that *labeling* an event `best_effort` discharges the obligation.** XD-6 already notes consumers can aggregate the honesty label away. But the deeper unexamined point: a regulated auditor does not accept "we honestly told you we don't know" as evidence of a *control operating effectively*. An honest gap is still a gap. The platform's defensibility claim ("legal defensibility", C-summary decision 2) assumes a labeled-unknown is better than a clean-but-false verdict — true — but slides into assuming it is *good enough for the audit* — unproven. **Falsifier:** put the `best_effort`-heavy replay in front of a real PCAOB/SOC2 auditor and ask whether a control whose evidence is 60% `best_effort` passes. If "honest about the gap" still fails the audit, the differentiator is integrity theater for the buyer's actual job.

## 3. What is missing entirely — capabilities/risks NO component owns

I searched the whole `spec-plan/` tree for owners of each of these. None has a home.

- **Production scale / cost / performance at anything beyond §22.** F3 SPEC §22 sizes the POC at **10k-500k events/day (~6/sec, 30-75 GB/30d)** and explicitly says "functional validation, not throughput" (R-F3-PERF-4). That is fine for a POC — but **no document anywhere models the platform at production scale** (a real enterprise K8s fleet is 10s-100s of millions of admission events/day). The append-only hash-chained, signed-Merkle, content-addressed-blob evidence store (C2 §7) has per-event cryptographic cost and unbounded storage growth, and *nobody* has costed it at 1000× the POC. MASTER-PLAN-ALT line 327 even forbids parallelizing E1's differential engine ("tight, serial, single-author core") — a serial core is a scale ceiling nobody quantified. **No owner.**
- **Multi-tenancy isolation beyond query scoping.** D2 scopes *reads/writes by predicate*. There is no compute isolation, no per-tenant storage isolation, no noisy-neighbor protection, no per-tenant rate limiting, no blast-radius bound when one tenant's policy evaluation or replay job saturates shared workers. The word "multi-tenant" appears only in the *sequencing* memos ("single-tenant first, defer real D2"). A scope predicate is not tenant isolation; a shared evidence log with a WHERE clause is one bug from cross-tenant disclosure. **No owner.**
- **Disaster recovery / RPO / RTO for the evidence store.** The corpus's only RTO is the *fail-closed break-glass* RTO (XD-7, D4). For the system of record — the tamper-evident audit log that is the entire product — there is **no backup strategy, no RPO, no DR, no restore-and-prove-the-chain-still-verifies story.** If the append-only log is lost, every downstream verdict, evaluation, and signed export is unbacked. For a compliance evidence product this is the most important durability property and it is unowned.
- **Audit-log retention economics.** C4-OQ2 bounds sweeps by "C2 raw retention (≥30d POC)". A real compliance regime (SOC2, financial services) requires **multi-year** retention. Raw external-data values + before/after state + request objects + jwt_claims, content-addressed and signed, retained for 7 years at production volume, is a large and unmodeled cost — and XD-1's resolution *increases* it by making value capture a MUST. **No owner, no cost model.**
- **Key management for all the signing.** Everything tamper-evident rests on the ed25519 signing key (C2 §7, XD-19 even names key compromise as unmodeled). **D4-OQ-1 explicitly leaves signing technology "unspecified."** There is no key-rotation procedure, no HSM/KMS requirement, no key-custody/separation-of-duties for the signer, no story for "the signing key was compromised — re-sign 5 years of evidence with what authority?" XD-19 flags it as "Hardening" and defers it. For a product whose *only* tamper-evidence is that key, key management is not hardening — it is the foundation. **No real owner.**
- **Schema migration in production.** C2 is "additive-only within v1.x" — but the corpus's *own* central finding (XD-3) is that v1.0 needs a **breaking** change (new `disposition` field replacing the conflated `decision` semantics). There is a one-line "major bump + migration plan" (Contract 1 §1.3) but **no actual migration plan exists** for re-normalizing already-stored frozen events, re-chaining the hash chain across a schema change, or re-signing checkpoints. The very first thing that happens to this schema (the rc pass) is the scenario nobody planned for.
- **Human factors — who actually writes the Rego.** The product's value depends on controls being expressed as Rego with correct `__control_id__`/`__required_claims__`/metadata. **No persona, no component, and no plan owns Rego authoring, the Rego learning curve, or what happens when the compliance team (Priya/Daniel) cannot write Rego and the platform team won't.** This is the #1 reason OPA-based governance products stall in the field, and it is invisible in the corpus (my search for "who writes Rego / author / learning curve" returned nothing in the product specs). The whole differentiation rests on a population of fluent Rego authors the corpus never staffs.
- **Operational burden of running the stack.** The MVP is ~14 components (MASTER-PLAN §5.1): Keycloak, OPA, Gatekeeper, Conftest, an operator + 9 CRDs, an append-only signed evidence store, an analytics engine, a simulation engine, a Headlamp plugin, a REST API, Privateer. **Nobody owns the run-cost, the on-call surface, the upgrade choreography across 14 interdependent services, or the SRE headcount.** For the regulated buyer this *is* the buying decision, and the corpus treats "deployment" (F2) as install-time only, never day-2.

## 5. Blunt verdict

**Is the corpus build-ready after the build-blocking fixes? No — not as written, because the "build-blocking fixes" themselves are contradicted by the very documents that are supposed to schedule them.** The CROSSCUT-ADVERSARIAL §4 build-blocking list is excellent and correct. But `MASTER-PLAN.md` (the doc a build team actually executes), `INTER-DOMAIN-CONTRACTS.md` (the doc that "wins" on conflicts, per its own §0), and `DATA-MODEL.md` all still encode the *pre*-fix world and in one case (INTER-DOMAIN-CONTRACTS §5.5) actively forbid the fix. You cannot "land the 9 build-blocking fixes and then build" when your authoritative plan and your authoritative contracts say the fixes aren't needed. **That is a deeper structural problem than any single XD defect — it is a reconciliation that wasn't reconciled.**

**Is it a *deep* structural problem in the architecture itself? No.** The architecture survives, as CROSSCUT-ADVERSARIAL says. The bones are good: a single `control_id` join key, a single identity edge, a single integrity primitive, an honesty-first evidence model, and a genuinely novel differential-simulation core. The defects are real but they are *correctible contract bugs*, not a wrong shape. The deeper problem is **process + missing NFRs**, not topology:
1. **One Wave-3 reconciliation pass** must make all six cross-cutting docs agree on `C2 v1.0-rc` (fixes M-1 / Risk 1). Until that pass exists, the corpus is internally inconsistent and not safe to hand to builders.
2. **A non-functional layer is entirely missing** — scale/cost, DR, key management, retention economics, multi-tenant isolation, Rego human-factors, day-2 ops (Risks 2-4, 7, 9, 10). The corpus is a *functional* architecture with no *operational* architecture. For a 5-10-year compliance-evidence product sold to regulated enterprises, the operational architecture is at least half the product, and it is absent.
3. **Two load-bearing claims are unproven** — cross-engine portability (G-1/Risk 6) and authoritative-replay coverage (G-2/Risk 5). Both are gating differentiators and both need an empirical test *before* the build commits, not after.

**Would I fund it?** **Conditionally, and not on the platform-first plan as written.** I would fund a **wedge-first** thin slice (MASTER-PLAN-ALT, which the market memos actually favor — G-5) that proves the *two* unproven claims (portability and replay-coverage) on real customer data with a paying design partner, *after* the one-pass cross-cutting reconciliation lands and *after* a one-page operational-architecture spike answers scale-cost, DR, and key-management at order-of-magnitude. I would **not** fund building all 14 MVP components on spec under a plan whose own market research calls platform-first "the bet the research says is weakest" and whose own scheduling docs contradict its own adversarial conclusions. The engineering talent here is evident and the corpus is unusually self-aware — but self-awareness that stays trapped in the adversarial document and never propagates to the plan is not yet a fundable plan. Fix the propagation, add the operational layer, prove the two claims, and it becomes one.
